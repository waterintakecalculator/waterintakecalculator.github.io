Calculate your daily water goal

Formula used: weight (kg) × 35 ml for adults (× 30 ml under 18), plus activity: sedentary 0 ml, light 350 ml, moderate 700 ml, active 1,050 ml, athlete 1,400 ml; plus climate: cool 0 ml, moderate 0 ml, hot 350 ml, very hot 700 ml; plus pregnancy 300 ml or breastfeeding 700 ml; minus 10% for age 56+. The result is rounded to the nearest 10 ml. This is a general estimate, not a medical prescription.

Recommended daily total fluids by group

These are the U.S. National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine Adequate Intake values for total water from all drinks and food, not plain water alone. About 20% usually comes from food.

Why the “8 glasses a day” rule is wrong for most people

Eight 8-ounce glasses is about 1.9 litres. It is a useful starting point, but it does not account for body size, exercise, heat, pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Signs you may not be drinking enough

  • Dark yellow urine — aim for pale straw colour
  • Afternoon fatigue and brain fog
  • Frequent headaches, dry mouth, or muscle cramps
  • Thirst mistaken for hunger

When you need more water than usual

  • Exercise, especially in heat
  • Hot weather, high altitude, or air travel
  • Illness with fever, vomiting or diarrhoea
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • High-protein diets

Can you drink too much water?

Yes. Drinking more than about 1 litre per hour for several hours can dilute blood sodium and cause hyponatremia, which can be serious. Spread your intake across the day and do not gulp very large volumes at once.

Tips to reach your goal

  • Drink a glass soon after waking
  • Carry a 1-litre bottle and track refills
  • Drink with meals and during exercise
  • Eat water-rich foods such as cucumber, watermelon and soup
  • Replace one sugary drink a day with water

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I drink a day?

Most adult men need about 3.7 L and women about 2.7 L of total fluids daily. Use the calculator for your personal number.

Is 8 glasses of water a day enough?

For a smaller, sedentary adult, yes. For larger adults, athletes, or hot climates, you will likely need more.

How is daily water intake calculated?

See the formula below the calculator. It starts with body weight and adds activity, climate, pregnancy or breastfeeding adjustments.

Do coffee, tea and juice count toward water intake?

Yes. Aim for at least 60% of daily fluid from plain water.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes. More than about 1 litre per hour for several hours can be harmful. Spread intake evenly through the day.
